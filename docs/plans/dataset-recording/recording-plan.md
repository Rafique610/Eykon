# Eykon — our own clips: recording plan and scripts

For Wednesday 14 October 2026, on campus, with two or three friends.
About 52 minutes of footage in 54 clips; about 3 hours of filming in two sessions.
A shorter version (about 70 minutes of filming) is at the end.

## Why we are recording

Everything measured so far rests on six short clips of our own and eight kitchen videos from
EPIC-KITCHENS. These recordings are meant to answer what those cannot:

| What we need to measure | Card | What the clips must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Finding small personal things | EYKO-32 | At least 30 moments of putting down or picking up keys, wallet, phone, glasses and so on |
| "Where is it **now**?" | EYKO-15 | The same object seen in two sessions, sometimes in the same place and sometimes moved |
| Choosing which frames to keep | EYKO-46 | Long, mostly still clips with a few short events at known times |
| Comparing the three ways of describing a frame | EYKO-33 | Many places, actions and objects, with answers we know for certain |
| A test nobody has tuned on | all | Some clips sealed away and not opened until the final test |

## Before you start

**People.** Three is enough: the **camera** person, one or two **actors**, and a **logger** who
fills in the sheet and keeps a stopwatch. Swap roles between sets so more than one person
appears on camera.

**Consent.** Ask every friend who appears: the clips are for a university research project, stay
on our own phone and laptop, and may be shown to the supervisor and panel. Do not film strangers
up close; in the cafeteria choose a corner or an angle where other people are small or behind.
Keep other people's screens and papers out of view.

**The phone.**

- Use the phone's own Camera app, 1080p, 30 frames a second, normal lens (1x), no zoom, no filters.
- **Landscape** for every clip (the phone held sideways), the same way round each time.
- Hold it at chest or eye height, as if it were a pair of glasses looking where you look. Move
  the way you normally would; do not try to be extra smooth.
- Check free space first: the whole plan needs about 8 GB.
- **Do not send the clips through WhatsApp.** It shrinks them and removes the recording time,
  which we need. Copy them from the phone with a cable, or upload the original files to Drive.

**The objects.** Bring these and keep the same ones all day:

| Name in the scripts | What it is |
|---|---|
| Keys A | Your own keys |
| Keys B | A friend's keys that look different |
| Phone A, Phone B | Two phones that look different (colour or case) |
| Wallet | One wallet |
| Glasses | Spectacles or sunglasses |
| Earbuds | An earbuds case |
| Charger | A charger with its cable |
| Pen A, Pen B | Two pens that look different |
| Bottle | A water bottle |

Also useful: a laptop, a backpack, a book, a cup.

**At the start of every clip** say the clip's name out loud, for example "Set one, clip three,
corridor". It makes matching files to the sheet easy and gives us speech to test later.

**Sealed clips.** Clips marked 🔒 are the final test. Record them like the others, log them, and
then nobody watches them or runs anything on them until the end. Do not skip them.

**The log sheet.** One row per clip in `log-template.csv` (same folder as this plan), or on
paper with the same columns. The most important columns are where each object was **left** and
the time of each event. Without the sheet the clips cannot be scored.

## Set 1 — putting things down and picking them up (10 clips, about 55 s each)

*For:* small objects, and the first sightings of each object.

Every clip follows the same steps:

1. Say the clip name. Look slowly around the place for 5 seconds.
2. For each object in "put down", in order: hold it up in front of the camera for 2 seconds, say
   what you are doing ("I am putting my keys on the desk"), put it down, take your hand right
   away, keep looking at it for 2 seconds, then look away.
3. For each object in "pick up": look at it for 2 seconds, say it ("I am picking up my phone"),
   pick it up, hold it in view for 2 seconds, then put it in your pocket or bag.
4. Look at the surface once more for 2 seconds, so what was left behind is seen.

| Clip | Place and surface | Put down, in this order | Pick up at the end | Left behind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1-01 | Classroom, front desk | Keys A, Phone A, Pen A | Phone A, Pen A | Keys A |
| S1-02 | Classroom, window sill | Wallet, Glasses, Earbuds | Wallet, Earbuds | Glasses |
| S1-03 🔒 | Corridor, bench | Keys B, Charger, Bottle | Bottle, Keys B | Charger |
| S1-04 | Cafeteria, table | Phone B, Wallet, Pen B | Phone B, Wallet | Pen B |
| S1-05 | Library or lab, desk | Glasses, Charger, Keys A | Glasses, Charger | Keys A |
| S1-06 🔒 | Lab desk, beside a laptop | Earbuds, Pen A, Phone A | Earbuds, Phone A | Pen A |
| S1-07 | Outside, bench or low wall | Bottle, Keys B, Wallet | Keys B, Wallet | Bottle |
| S1-08 | Inside a parked car (seat or dashboard), or on stairs | Phone A, Glasses, Charger | Charger, Phone A | Glasses |
| S1-09 🔒 | Canteen or shop counter | Wallet, Pen B, Earbuds | Wallet, Pen B | Earbuds |
| S1-10 | Any desk, with a backpack on it | Keys A, Charger, Pen A, each **into a pocket of the bag** | Charger, Keys A (out of the bag) | Pen A, inside the bag |

That is 50 moments in all, 35 of them in clips that are not sealed. After each clip the logger
collects what was left behind.

## Set 2 — the same things, seen again later (8 clips, about 30 s each)

*For:* the "where is it now" rule. No hands here: the objects are already lying in place.

Record the four A clips in the first session and the four B clips in the second, **at least two
hours later**. If any of you is on campus on Thursday morning, recording the B clips then is
better still, because the rule is meant to work across days.

In each clip: say the name, walk up to the object, look at it from about 1 metre for 5 seconds,
step back to about 2 metres for 3 seconds, look around the place, leave.

## Set 3 — long quiet clips with a few events (4 clips, 5 minutes each)

*For:* choosing which frames to keep. Most of each clip should be boring on purpose.

Prop the phone against books, or hold it loosely and stay put. The logger starts a stopwatch
with the recording and writes the real time of each event (mm:ss). The times below are only a guide.

| Clip | Scene | Events |
|---|---|---|
| S3-01 | Classroom desk, one person studying | 1:00 a friend sits down opposite · 2:00 puts the Bottle on the desk · 3:00 opens a laptop · 4:00 picks up the Bottle and leaves |
| S3-02 | Looking down a corridor | 0:45 someone walks past · 2:00 two people stop and talk for 15 s · 3:30 someone puts a bag on the bench · 4:30 takes it away |
| S3-03 🔒 | Cafeteria table | 1:00 food or a cup is put down · 2:30 Phone B is put on the table · 3:30 a friend joins · 4:30 everything is picked up |
| S3-04 | Standing outside, phone in the hand | 1:00 a person or car passes · 2:30 a friend hands you Keys A · 4:00 you put the keys in your pocket |

## Set 4 — walking through places (6 clips, 60 to 90 s each)

*For:* naming the place, and how things look while moving.

Walk the route at a normal pace. At each of the three stops stand still for 5 seconds and look
slowly from left to right. The logger notes the time you reach each stop.

| Clip | Route |
|---|---|
| S4-01 | Classroom → corridor → stairs |
| S4-02 | Corridor → cafeteria → canteen or shop counter |
| S4-03 🔒 | Library or lab → corridor → a teacher's office or department office (door is enough if you may not go in) |
| S4-04 | Building exit → path or road → parking |
| S4-05 | Parking → sit inside a car for 10 s → get out |
| S4-06 🔒 | Lawn or open ground → building entrance → classroom |

## Set 5 — one action per clip (16 clips, 20 to 25 s each)

*For:* naming what someone is doing. Film a friend from about 2 metres. Each action is done
twice, by two different people (take 1 and take 2). Start recording, wait 3 seconds, do the
action for about 15 seconds, wait 3 seconds, stop.

| Clips (take 1, take 2) | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| S5-01, S5-02 | Picking something up | A book or bag from a desk; repeat three times |
| S5-03 🔒, S5-04 | Putting something down | The same, the other way |
| S5-05, S5-06 🔒 | Typing on a laptop | Seated at a desk |
| S5-07, S5-08 | Walking | Along a corridor, towards and then away from the camera |
| S5-09 🔒, S5-10 | Talking to someone | Two people facing each other |
| S5-11, S5-12 🔒 | Reading | A book or printed pages, seated |
| S5-13, S5-14 | Eating or drinking | In the cafeteria |
| S5-15 🔒, S5-16 | Writing on a board | Whiteboard or blackboard |

## Set 6 — hands without any of the listed objects (4 clips, about 45 s each)

*For:* making sure the system does not "find" keys or a phone that are not there. Film your own
hands, or a friend's from close by. None of the listed small objects (keys, wallet, phone,
glasses, earbuds, charger, pen) may be in view. Each clip has about eight hand moments, 4 to 5
seconds each.

| Clip | Hand moments |
|---|---|
| S6-01 | Typing on a laptop · turning the pages of a book · closing the book · moving the book aside |
| S6-02 | Holding a cup · drinking · putting the cup down · wiping the table |
| S6-03 🔒 | Opening a door · pressing a switch or lift button · zipping a bag · tying a shoelace |
| S6-04 | Talking with hand gestures · counting on fingers · clapping · pointing at the board with a marker |

## Set 7 — hard cases (6 clips, about 20 s each)

*For:* knowing honestly where it breaks. In each, Keys A and Phone A lie on a surface.

| Clip | What makes it hard |

## Afterwards

1. Copy the original files from the phone with a cable into `data/evals/own-2026-10-14/clips/`
   on the laptop (this folder is not in git). Rename each file to its clip name, for example
   `S1-03.mp4`.
2. Put the filled log sheet next to them as `log.csv`.
3. Move the sealed clips into `data/evals/own-2026-10-14/sealed/` without watching them.
4. Tell the assistant the clips are there. The questions with their answers ("Where are my keys
   now?" → S2-B1, classroom, front desk) will be written from the log sheet, before anything
   is run.

## What these clips still will not prove

One campus, one day, a few friends, objects we chose and moments we staged. Staged moments are
clearer than real life. This is far better evidence than we have now, and it is still our own
data: the kitchen dataset stays as the outside check.
