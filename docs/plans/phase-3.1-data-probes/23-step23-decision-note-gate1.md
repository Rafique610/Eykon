# Phase 3.1 Step 23 — Decision Note and Gate 1

## What

Bring all of Phase 3.1 together into one short, honest document and one
meeting with the supervisor:

1. **Decision note** `docs/results/phase-3.1/decision-note.md`, in plain
   language, at most about 4 pages:
   - what data now exists (generated counts from `data-status.md` and
     `qa-status.md`),
   - Probe A, B, C: question, pre-registered threshold, measured result with
     interval, verdict, the most important failure, phone cost,
   - what was not done or came out unclear, and why,
   - the decision for each Gate 1 question, using the decision table written
     in Step 13,
   - the resulting scope for Phases 4.1, 5.1 and 5.2 (what is kept, cut or
     changed), and for the Phase 4 Step 09 two-path design,
   - which open decisions from the handoff doc §6 are now settled
     (detector strategy, primary VLM role, speaker separation model) and
     which remain open.
2. **Gate 1 review** with the supervisor: send the note in advance, discuss,
   record the outcome (agreed / agreed with changes / not agreed) and any
   changes in the note's last section.
3. **Plan annotations**: add a dated note to the Phase 4, 4.1, 5.1 and 5.2
   overviews stating what Gate 1 decided (annotation, nothing deleted).
4. **Thesis material**: a one-page "Phase 3.1 in one slide" summary for the
   re-defense package (Phase 3 Step 13) and later the thesis.

## Why

- Gate 1 is the first point where the project's direction is decided by
  measured evidence instead of by plan (roadmap v2 §6).
- The panel asked whether an LLM is needed. This note is the first, partial,
  evidence-based answer, and it must be readable by someone who did not
  follow the work.
- Writing negative or unclear results down (and what we do because of them)
  is the pessimist rule in action. A "no" here is a result, not a failure.
- Annotating the later phase docs keeps plans honest: anyone opening the
  Phase 4.1 plan sees immediately whether it was kept, shrunk or dropped.

## How to implement

1. **Generate, don't type, the numbers**: a script
   `src/probes/decision_tables.py` pulls the headline numbers from the three
   probe reports and the status files into the note's tables, so the note
   cannot drift from the data.
2. **Write the narrative** around those tables: one paragraph per probe,
   then the decisions, then the scope changes.
3. **Decision table** (from Step 13), filled:

   | Gate 1 question | Result | Verdict | Consequence |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | VLM invention rate acceptable? | from Probe A | acceptable / unclear / unacceptable | VLM path role in Phase 4 Step 09 and Phase 5 state table |
   | Cheap hand-centred method viable? | from Probe B | viable / unclear / not viable | Phase 4.1 full / reduced / dropped |
   | Speaker separation usable? | from Probe C | usable / partly / unusable | Phase 5.1 who-said-what / me-vs-others / low-confidence labels |

4. **Supervisor meeting**: 15–20 minutes, note in advance, outcome written
   down the same day.
5. **Annotate** the later phase overviews and the master roadmap's v2
   annotation block with the Gate 1 outcome and date.
6. **Phase review**: list every step's status, open issues carried forward,
   and anything flagged but not fixed during the phase (the running list in
   the phase overview).

## Open decisions

- **What if the supervisor disagrees with a verdict?** Options: (a) run the
  allowed extension once (more frames / events / minutes, as pre-registered),
  then decide; (b) accept the supervisor's direction and record the
  disagreement and the evidence; (c) keep both directions alive for one more
  phase at reduced scope — costs time, avoids an early wrong cut.
- **How to present "unclear" results to the panel.** Options: (a) as
  unclear, with the planned follow-up — honest; (b) folded into the
  limitations section — less visible; (c) as a future-work item only. The
  roadmap's honesty rules favour (a).

## You test this

1. Read the decision note as if you were on the panel. Mark every sentence
   that you could not defend if asked "how do you know?".
2. Explain the three Gate 1 decisions out loud in under 2 minutes, without
   the note.
3. After the supervisor meeting, check that the outcome section matches what
   was actually said.

## How this number could be lying

- The note summarises; summaries drop caveats. Each headline number keeps
  its interval, sample size and "laptop / phone" label, and links to the full
  report's "how this number could be lying" section.
- Agreement with the supervisor can be read as validation of the numbers. It
  is a decision about direction, not proof the numbers are right.

## Common issues

- Waiting for a perfect result before writing the note. Write it with what
  exists; "unclear" is an allowed verdict.
- The supervisor is unavailable for weeks. Send the note by email and
  proceed with the pre-registered decisions, recording that the review is
  pending.
